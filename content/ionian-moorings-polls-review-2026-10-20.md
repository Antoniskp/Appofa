## Business assessment

The likely commercial model is to install and maintain moorings, then charge visiting leisure vessels for their use. This is an inference from the reported applications and registered activity; no company business plan was available.

Revenue depends on usable berths, operating days, occupancy and realised daily price. For scale only, using the **reported 172 places**:

| Illustrative scenario | Operating days | Occupancy | Assumed daily revenue per occupied berth, excluding VAT | Seasonal berth revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 120 | 35% | €20 | €144,480 |
| Middle | 150 | 55% | €30 | €425,700 |
| High | 180 | 75% | €45 | €1,044,900 |

These are arithmetic scenarios, **not company prices, forecasts or profit estimates**. Formula: places × days × occupancy × price. They exclude installation, surveys, licensing, sea-space charges, staff, service boats, insurance, inspections, repairs, payment fees, taxes, financing and removal. Without those costs, neither profitability nor payback can be assessed.

The commercial attraction is recurring seasonal revenue in desirable bays. The central public-interest question is how much existing free anchoring is displaced and what users and residents receive in exchange.

Article 12 of the 2025 framework prohibits own-anchor anchoring by the specified tourist vessels and small craft inside an installed mooring area and within 50 metres around it. That is an anchoring restriction; it should not be described as a general ban on swimming or public ownership of the sea changing hands. Article 10 requires maintenance, inspections and publication of technical details and tariffs. Exact effects require the proposed boundary maps. [Official Gazette B 3117/2025, articles 10–12](https://api.et.gr/apiFEK/2/2025/3117/pdf)

Potential value includes organised berthing, clearer capacity management and a funded maintenance service. Specially designed moorings can reduce interaction with sensitive seabeds, but ordinary moorings are not automatically environmentally beneficial. Site design, habitat mapping and monitoring matter; no such benefit has been established for this proposal. [Natural England, advanced mooring systems guidance](https://publications.naturalengland.org.uk/publication/6689623831740416)

Business risks include permission delays, local opposition, weather exposure, maintenance liability and customers choosing other bays. Community risks include displacement of existing uses, unaffordable prices, boat concentration and inadequate local benefits. Conversely, local suppliers and visitor businesses might gain from better-managed visits. These are scenarios to evaluate, not measured outcomes.

Before judging the investment, seek: ownership and relevant experience; full application maps; habitat and capacity assessments; emergency access; tariffs and resident/fishing arrangements; public revenue terms; maintenance and insurance commitments; removal security; and the company's response to local objections.

## Poll design

Recommend **four separate, single-choice polls**: two about verified reported applications, one about the preferred management model, and one about municipal consent. This avoids interpreting opposition to a particular operator or scale as opposition to every possible mooring scheme.

Use public visibility, authenticated voting, comments enabled and results after voting. These are non-representative participation polls, not official municipal consultations or scientific estimates. Open participation does not establish that voters are residents. Do not label the results “what Meganisi residents want” without a separately verified resident sample.

Appofa supports `simple` polls and these settings. Keep answer contributions disabled so the choices remain comparable. Suggested initial duration: 14 days from publication. Link each local poll to its verified location record when publishing; no database IDs have been invented.

## Editorial notes before publication

Recheck application status and include source links alongside the descriptions. Keep the business revenue scenarios in the analysis rather than in the voting introductions: they are hypothetical and could anchor respondents to invented prices.

Publish the two local polls separately; never pool their votes into a single island-community result. Keep the company-specific questions separate from the policy questions. Avoid “selling the sea”, “ecological investment” or similar conclusions in titles unless directly attributed in supporting reporting.

If an Ithaca application becomes verifiable, prepare a separate local poll using its actual applicant, bays and status. Do not simply substitute “Ιθάκη” into the Meganisi description.
